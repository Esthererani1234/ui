# Gold On The Spot tax preview

## What customers can try

Open `/tax-preview` on the preview deployment to search all 50 states and Washington, DC, read the government guidance, and try sample orders for five minimums and six taxable destinations. Select Washington, Hawaii, Vermont or New Mexico to enter a street address. The sample-order panel provides a CSV of the geographic rate records. A qualifying New York investment bar priced at exactly $1,000 needs another cent under the example store minimum. Two Washington regression addresses share ZIP 98501. For Q4 2026, a taxable $20,000 purchase calculates $2,000 at the Olympia address and $1,960 at the Tumwater address. These examples use fictional transactions and never reserve inventory or initiate payment.

Checkout keeps all 50 states available for shipping. It displays a minimum notice only when the eligible order is short. The store floors are $1,000.01 for New York and Maryland, $2,000 for California, and $1,000 for Massachusetts and Connecticut. Connecticut's current floor ends on June 30, 2027. New Jersey has a product-specific coin test. A positive customer charge appears as one **Tax** line. Zero, exempt, pending and noncollection statuses have no customer tax row. Failed verification is handled before payment; it never produces an assumed zero. New York investment purchasers confirm their purchase purpose in the existing agreement.

## Washington remote sales in the checkout preview

For this store's confirmed NYC-only operations and direct website sales, a Washington delivery can be taxable merchandise with no sales tax collected. The preview checks the Washington registration profile, earlier receipts, open orders and known off-site activity. Exceeding $100,000 of Washington receipts in a calendar year starts a registration and B&O review. The first collection date is the first of the month at least 30 days after the threshold crossing. For example, a first $500,000 sale on November 26, 2026 points to January 1, 2027. The November sale does not carry a customer tax charge on those facts. A registered seller whose collection date has arrived uses the Washington street-rate lookup. The rule is reviewed through December 31, 2026. [Washington Department of Revenue](https://dor.wa.gov/taxes-rates/retail-sales-tax/marketplace-fairness-leveling-playing-field/remote-sellers)

Alabama, Florida, Michigan, New Mexico and Rhode Island use prior-calendar-year tests. With a confirmed nonregistered profile, the November release can determine that a first delivery to one of these five states requires no collection. It checks the NYC-only merchant history against older orders and imported sales. Unclassified merchandise is reported separately, including through partial refunds. A conflicting address, registration, historical sale, or expired review prevents the determination. The preview dashboard flags current-year direct website activity for next year's registration review separately from prior-year activity. The amount displayed is a screening measure, since each state's threshold may include different sales.

The order-level review also covers 30 remote destinations where the retailer's sales can trigger collection. It compares receipts and transaction counts against the published thresholds, includes the proposed purchase, shipping and the largest applicable payment fee, and counts open orders and unresolved destinations conservatively. Below the limits, a verified nonregistered seller can receive a noncollection determination. A crossing, an off-site sale, a physical location or an active registration requires the applicable collection policy. Order placement serializes final checks by destination.

## Delivery address correction

The cart, checkout and account address editor use a nationwide street lookup. A street plus ZIP can fill missing city and state. Equivalent street abbreviations are standardized and unit information is preserved. Customers confirm changes to existing names or ZIP codes. Competing matches or different house numbers require an edit. Payments, the order, a saved default address and the tax assessment use the same confirmed destination. Pickup uses the fixed office address.

The preview at `/tax-preview#delivery-address` allows a read-only test using the [US Census Geocoder](https://www.census.gov/data/developers/data-sets/Geocoding-services.html). Its street ranges establish a geographic match. Apartment occupancy and carrier deliverability are outside that coverage. In an authorized USPS shipping integration, `USPS_ADDRESS_ENABLED=true` with server-only `USPS_CLIENT_ID` and `USPS_CLIENT_SECRET` selects [USPS Addresses](https://developers.usps.com/addressesv3) for delivery-point confirmation. The preview uses Census and has no USPS credentials. The system never treats an unanswered lookup as a successful postal verification.

## Checkout and accounting

- Delivery uses a full street address. Pickup uses the store’s New York address. Cart estimates and final checkout use the same calculation service. Discounts are allocated across merchandise, and reviewed rules determine how shipping and payment charges affect the taxable base.
- The server binds the assessment to the customer, price lock, payment method, fulfillment, address and rule revision. The order stores the assessment with the calculated total. Missing or expired approvals block an affected checkout.
- The calculation supports separate qualifying merchandise, voluntary store minimums, price-to-metal-value limits and individual coin tests. Government rate components and seller-level gross receipts or excise charges have separate ledger fields.
- Admin → Sales Tax & Compliance provides state research, registrations, source and product review, private evidence, nexus alerts, administrative filing schedules, payment confirmations, and a period ledger CSV. Refund entries require a confirmed reference and are capped by the original tax components.
- A filing or remittance enters the ledger only after an administrator records the actual event. Reconcile earlier orders, other selling channels, refunds and tax payments against business records before using totals for returns.

A merchant can record **Not Registered** for an enabled shipping destination. The checkout still requires a reviewed collection decision for that destination. Registration status alone cannot produce a zero-tax quote.

For New York's investment exemption, the current catalog has four gold bars (PAMP, APMEX, kilogram and Rand), three one-ounce gold coins (Buffalo Proof, Eagle and Maple Leaf), and one Silver Maple Leaf. The price ceilings for otherwise eligible items are 115% for those bars and gold coins and 140% for the silver coin. The Buffalo Proof requires its own review of qualifying use and price. Compare each bar with the cash value of its fine metal content, using the actual kilogram weight for the kilo bar. For coins, compare with the greater of that metal value and the face value converted to USD. Use the preceding business day’s closing cash price or its bid/ask average when no closing price exists. Record its observation date and reviewed market calendar for the sale date. The storefront's marked-up spot quote cannot supply this benchmark. [New York Tax Law § 1115(a)(27)](https://www.nysenate.gov/legislation/laws/TAX/1115)

## Exact-address reconciliation

For a $500,000 taxable wire purchase with no additional charges and an obligated seller, the Spokane City Hall address (location 3210) yields $32,500 state plus $13,000 local, a $545,500 total. Seattle City Hall (1726) yields $32,500 state plus $20,250 local, a $552,750 total. Both were checked using DOR address responses, the official street-range archive and the Q4 2026 published location rates. The calculator supports both addresses. All 403 Washington location codes have county and reporting-area labels from the state workbook. The ledger preserves state and combined-local amounts. It also records the transit district indicators returned by DOR. The published aggregate local rate cannot be apportioned across individual districts from those files alone.

New York City general-rate transactions can use a full street match confirmed in both a NYC borough and the incorporated city. The components are state, city and MCTD. Product exemptions and the company's collection status are reviewed separately. Outside NYC the server matches the county and any separately reported city to Publication 718. In a county with city exceptions, conflicting or incomplete boundary evidence requires review. Billing, wire instructions and issued invoices use the tax-inclusive stored order total. A card transaction remains subject to the existing payment limits.

Sale and refund components are summarized on the administration state's reporting-period view. Unreviewed jurisdictions, historical sales, and registration status still require the evidence described below. No production collection rules were activated.

## Verification and production decision

The 367-test suite includes 106 tax and delivery-address tests. Of those, 51 check that each unapproved jurisdiction blocks calculation. Other cases cover the New York cent boundary, qualification and premium limits, New Jersey coins, mixed baskets and rounding, the Washington rate parser and sample, and missing data. A local PostgreSQL-compatible database exercises the migration, payment posting, repeat events, partial refunds, nexus and filing records. Boundary tests cover all five store floors and positive customer charges for the six example destinations. The full repository suite also checks checkout, payments, account, support and email behavior and a production build.

The test evidence covers implemented behavior. Comprehensive comparisons against independently verified legal examples for every state and locality, a full government data import, and an isolated hosted checkout-to-receipt test for each payment method are outstanding. The hosted public preview contains examples. The transactional tests used a local database. The private tax database is installed in production. Customer checkout activation is a separate migration. Production activation requires verified rates and product policies, business-specific registration and history, end-to-end payment tests on an isolated database, and the owner's rollout decision.
