Six distinct stages 1. Displayed spot reference: indicative market information, not an executable offer or the final merchandise price. Submitted order: your affirmative submission before quote expiration offers to buy the identified goods on the displayed terms. The receipt must identify its offer-expiry time. Proposed offer window: until the quoted ten-minute expiry; a later acceptance requires a new quote and new assent. You may withdraw an unaccepted offer through the order portal or support, subject to recording which event occurred first. No market-loss charge applies to a withdrawn, expired or rejected unaccepted offer. 4. Company acceptance: the Company issues a definitive notice titled “Order accepted — binding purchase agreement,” records its issuance, and makes it available in your account. The notice identifies the order, goods, quantity, total, agreement versions and payment deadline. It must match your still-open offer. Market movements alone do not entitle either party to cancel. This stage is distinct from payment clearance. 6. Paid and verified order: trusted payment confirmation and required review permit fulfillment. A payment screen, bank screenshot or submitted wire advice is not proof of cleared payment. Quote versus contract A displayed market reference is indicative. A temporary server quote lasts ten minutes and applies only to its identified account, products, quantities, destination and payment method. It cannot be transferred, extended by browser changes or reused for another order. Submission alone does not make the purchase binding. Only definitive Company acceptance of a still-open offer creates the mutual price obligation described in the Purchase Agreement.

When damages may arise If you breach an accepted agreement without a lawful excuse, we may seek losses caused by that breach that applicable law and the agreement permit. We must establish the contract, breach, causal loss and a valid measure of damages; review any defenses; avoid unnecessary loss; and credit amounts saved or recovered. Ordinary market movement before acceptance creates no default claim. We cannot charge a loss caused by our own inability to supply, unjustified rejection or delayed mitigation.

Calculation and review A commercially reasonable resale measure ordinarily compares the accepted merchandise price for the affected quantity with the documented resale proceeds, adds recoverable incidental expenses and subtracts saved expenses and applicable credits. The legal suitability, timing, notice and allocation of that resale must be reviewed. If a different statutory measure is necessary, counsel must select it and identify the relevant market, time and place. A percentage change in spot alone is not the bill.

Documented hedging or administrative expenditures are considered only if caused by the breach, reasonably incurred and independently recoverable; they must not duplicate a price differential or ordinary overhead already included. Collection costs and attorneys’ fees are not automatic: recovery requires an applicable legal basis and appropriate approval or award. No fixed or percentage default fee is adopted by this draft. A future liquidated-damages proposal requires a separate reasonableness analysis and assent; it cannot be added to an old order retroactively.

Illustrative example — not an automatic formula Suppose an accepted merchandise price is $20,000. After an actionable customer default, a properly documented, commercially reasonable resale produces $19,400. Assume $80 of allowable incremental expense and $30 of costs avoided, with no other applicable credit. The candidate loss is $650 ($20,000 − $19,400 + $80 − $30). It remains subject to legal review of causation, mitigation, notice, evidence and offsets. A $1,000 payment already held is not automatically forfeited; any lawful $650 offset would leave $350 to return, subject to the actual legal restitution analysis. If the comparable resale instead produces $20,100, there is no $600 price-difference loss to invoice. Any remaining claimed expense must be separately justified and cannot create double recovery.

A defaulting purchaser does not automatically receive a speculative market gain or a right to direct the Company’s inventory resale. This does not eliminate mandatory restitution, required credits or an accounting for customer funds. We do not promise a positive resale return.

Notice and dispute process We will provide a written explanation identifying the accepted order, default, mitigation action, reference prices or resale evidence, recoverable expenses, credits and claimed amount. Confidential counterparty data may be appropriately redacted without hiding the basis of the calculation. You may challenge the facts or calculation through support and request human review. We will not automatically debit a bank account or charge a saved card for alleged damages. Any later payment authorization must independently satisfy law and processor rules. Reasonable dispute handling must not become intimidation or restrict lawful regulatory, payment or court remedies.
